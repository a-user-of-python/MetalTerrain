Water Rendering

A single flat plane at sea level, animated entirely in the fragment shader. The geometry never moves — that's a deliberate, load-bearing design decision.

The flat-plane contract

Water is a constant, flat physical plane at sea level. It shows wherever terrain dips below it. The animated appearance lives only in the fragment-shader texture — the triangles never move. No foam, no shoreline effects, no geometric waves. This contract exists because moving water vertices causes shoreline glitching and z-fighting where water meets terrain.

  • Geometry: MTMeshBuilder.buildWaterMesh(size:level:) builds a flat 64×64-segment plane centered on the XZ origin. 64 segments is the locked value.
  • Level: worldY(forHeight: config.seaLevel) — the same sea level the terrain and biome logic use, so shorelines agree everywhere.
  • Size: (viewDistance × 2 + 4) × chunkWorldSize, rebuilt when the config changes.
  • Position: recentered under the camera target every frame via the model matrix, snapped to a grid of waterSize / 64 so vertices sit at stable world positions — this is what prevents shoreline swimming/jitter as the camera moves.

Draw order and blending

Water is drawn last, after skybox, terrain, and structures: blended (waterAlpha = 0.82, fixed), depth-tested but with no depth write. That ordering means:

  • Terrain above sea level correctly occludes water (depth test).
  • Water doesn't occlude things drawn after it (nothing is — it's last, except your overlay).
  • The sea floor shows through shallow water — that's the ocean/ beach biome colors doing their job, not transparency trickery.

The animated texture

What does move is the fragment-shader water texture: layered procedural waves and sun glints, all computed per-pixel from world position and time. Because it's a pure function of (worldXZ, time), the animation is stable — no swimming, no tiling artifacts, no texture memory.

API

// Toggle water rendering:
renderer.showsWater = true
terrainView.showsWater = false   // forwarded to the renderer

// Water color (linear RGB) and sea level live in the world config:
var config = MTTerrainConfig.default
config.waterColor = SIMD3<Float>(0.05, 0.30, 0.55)  // linear, not sRGB
config.seaLevel = 0.45                              // normalized 0...1
world.config = config   // rebuilds the water plane

// Gameplay: "can the player be here?" — water blocking:
func isLand(_ p: SIMD2<Float>, world: MTTerrainWorld) -> Bool {
    world.heightAt(x: Double(p.x), z: Double(p.y)) >= world.config.seaLevel
}

Shorelines

Shorelines emerge from three systems agreeing on one number (seaLevel = 0.45):

  1. The heightfield flattens toward a coastal shelf near sea level, so beaches read as beaches.
  2. The biome ladder has a dedicated beach band (0.40–0.52) overlapping the top of ocean.
  3. The water plane sits exactly at sea level, grid-snapped so the waterline doesn't crawl as you move.

If you change seaLevel, all three follow — but re-verify beach width on a few seeds, since the shelf flattening is tuned around 0.45.

What not to do

  • Don't displace water vertices. Ever. It breaks the shoreline.
  • Don't add foam unless the requirement changes — it's an explicit non-goal (it was tried and removed).
  • Don't raymarch the water. The flat plane + fragment texture is the whole technique; it's cheap and it looks right.
