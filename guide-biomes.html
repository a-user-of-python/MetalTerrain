Biome Authoring

Biomes turn raw heights into a world that reads — ocean vs. beach vs. forest vs. snow. This guide covers the mapping, the built-in table, and custom biome recipes.

How biomes map from height

Every point has a normalized height 0…1 (world.heightAt(x:z:)). A biome claims a slice of that range:

MTBiome(
    name: "grass",
    minHeight: 0.50,   // inclusive — heights >= 0.50 can match
    maxHeight: 0.65,   // exclusive — heights < 0.65 can match
    groundColor: SIMD3(0.25, 0.55, 0.20),  // linear RGB 0...1
    slopeColor: SIMD3(0.45, 0.38, 0.30),   // cliffs (optional)
    emitsLight: false
)

Key rules: minHeight is inclusive, maxHeight is exclusive. Stack ranges end-to-start for gapless coverage. Colors are linear RGB 0…1 (not sRGB 0–255). Biome colors bake into vertex colors at mesh build — don't animate them every frame.

The built-in table

MTBiome.default ships seven biomes tiling the full 0…1 range:

BiomeHeight rangeLook
deepOceanbottom of range → lowdark blue water floor
oceanlow → below sea levelmid blue water floor
beacharound seaLevel ≈ 0.45sand
grasslowlands above the beachgreen plains
forestrolling highlandsdarker, denser green
mountainhigh altitudegrey rock (with slopeColor cliffs)
snowyPeaktop of rangewhite

The beach band straddles config.seaLevel — raising sea level widens the ocean and narrows the land.

Custom biomes: the three operations

// Add a NEW biome (or REPLACE the one with the same name)
world.setBiome(MTBiome(name: "volcano", minHeight: 0.90, maxHeight: 1.0,
                       groundColor: SIMD3(0.15, 0.05, 0.05),
                       slopeColor: SIMD3(0.35, 0.10, 0.05),
                       emitsLight: true))

// Remove a custom biome you added (built-ins can't be removed individually)
world.removeBiome(named: "volcano")

// Throw away ALL custom biomes, restore the built-in table
world.resetBiomesToDefault()

Precedence rules

  1. Custom biomes are checked first, in insertion order. The first custom biome whose range contains the height wins.
  2. Built-ins are the fallback.
  3. setBiome with an existing name replaces in place — no duplicates.

A custom biome overlapping a built-in range shadows the built-in there — that's how you override snowyPeak.

Recipe 1: Higher mountains

// Snow starts lower and covers more of the highlands
world.setBiome(MTBiome(name: "snowyPeak",
                       minHeight: 0.80, maxHeight: 1.0,
                       groundColor: SIMD3(0.92, 0.94, 0.97),
                       slopeColor: SIMD3(0.55, 0.50, 0.58)))

// Stretch mountain down to meet it — no gap, no overlap
world.setBiome(MTBiome(name: "mountain",
                       minHeight: 0.68, maxHeight: 0.80,
                       groundColor: SIMD3(0.42, 0.40, 0.44),
                       slopeColor: SIMD3(0.30, 0.28, 0.32)))

Pair with noise.ridged = true and higher heightScale for jagged alpine terrain.

Recipe 2: Lava biome (with emitsLight)

world.setBiome(MTBiome(name: "snowyPeak",
                       minHeight: 0.88, maxHeight: 1.0,
                       groundColor: SIMD3(0.92, 0.94, 0.97),
                       slopeColor: SIMD3(0.55, 0.50, 0.58)))

// Lava fields between mountain and snow
world.setBiome(MTBiome(name: "lava",
                       minHeight: 0.80, maxHeight: 0.88,
                       groundColor: SIMD3(0.85, 0.18, 0.05),  // molten orange-red
                       slopeColor: SIMD3(0.25, 0.05, 0.03),   // cooled crust on cliffs
                       emitsLight: true))                     // glows in the shader

The fragment shader treats emitsLight vertices as self-illuminated — they skip NdotL darkening, so lava reads as glowing even at night. Tip: keep glowing bands narrow.

Recipe 3: Underwater biomes

// Tropical shallows: bright turquoise sand under the water
world.setBiome(MTBiome(name: "ocean",
                       minHeight: 0.15, maxHeight: 0.42,
                       groundColor: SIMD3(0.15, 0.65, 0.60)))

// Abyssal plain: near-black
world.setBiome(MTBiome(name: "deepOcean",
                       minHeight: 0.00, maxHeight: 0.15,
                       groundColor: SIMD3(0.01, 0.03, 0.06)))

The water plane (config.waterColor) is drawn translucent over these ground colors — the final look is the blend of the two.

Tips

  • Cover 0…1. Unmatched heights fall back to the built-in list.
  • Mind the sea. Keep water-adjacent bands near config.seaLevel.
  • Slope colors sell cliffs. Rock on mountains, dark loam in forests, pale sand on dunes.
  • Structures avoid some biomes — placement keeps to land between beach top and snow.
  • Name discipline: "SnowyPeak" ≠ "snowyPeak". Reuse exact built-in names when overriding.

The built-in biome ladder

Seven biomes, keyed on normalized height. Lookup is first match in array order — bands may overlap, and the earlier entry wins. Note the deliberate ocean/beach overlap at 0.40–0.45: heights just above sea level (0.45) read as beach, giving a natural shoreline band.

NameHeight bandGround colorSlope colorNotes
deepOcean0.00 – 0.32(0.02, 0.12, 0.30)—Never visible (under the water plane)
ocean0.32 – 0.45(0.05, 0.28, 0.55)—Sea floor near shores
beach0.40 – 0.52(0.85, 0.75, 0.55)—Overlaps ocean; wins above 0.40
grass0.52 – 0.62(0.25, 0.55, 0.20)—Plains and valleys
forest0.62 – 0.72(0.12, 0.38, 0.12)—Darker green foothills
mountain0.72 – 0.80(0.45, 0.42, 0.38)(0.32, 0.30, 0.28)Rock; cliffs go darker
snowyPeak0.80 – 1.00(0.90, 0.92, 0.95)(0.55, 0.56, 0.58)Snow; steep faces expose rock

Colors are linear RGB in 0…1, not sRGB. If you're picking colors from a design tool, convert: linear = pow(srgb, 2.2) approximately. Getting this wrong makes everything look washed out or muddy.

How lookup works

let biome = world.biomeAt(height: h)  // h in 0...1, clamped automatically

// What happens inside (simplified):
// 1. Custom biomes first, in the order you added them (setBiome)
// 2. Then config.biomes (the built-in ladder, unless you replaced it)
// 3. First biome with minHeight <= h < maxHeight wins
// 4. If NOTHING matches (empty lists), you get a flat-gray "void" biome —
//    a safety net, not a feature. Don't rely on it.

Custom biomes take precedence. world.setBiome(_:) inserts into a custom list consulted before config.biomes. To override grass, add a custom biome named "grass" with your band — it shadows the built-in. To start over, world.resetBiomesToDefault() clears customs.

Authoring custom biomes

Example: volcanic badlands

// Ashen flats where grass used to be, lava veins on steep slopes
world.setBiome(MTBiome(
    name: "ashenFlats",
    minHeight: 0.52, maxHeight: 0.66,
    groundColor: SIMD3<Float>(0.23, 0.20, 0.19),   // dark ash, linear RGB
    slopeColor: SIMD3<Float>(0.85, 0.25, 0.05)))   // glowing lava on cliffs

// A lava biome that actually emits light (bloom-ish in the shader)
world.setBiome(MTBiome(
    name: "lava",
    minHeight: 0.44, maxHeight: 0.48,
    groundColor: SIMD3<Float>(1.0, 0.30, 0.05),
    emitsLight: true))

Example: desert planet (replace the whole ladder)

var config = MTTerrainConfig.default
config.seaLevel = 0.38
config.biomes = [
    MTBiome(name: "deepOcean", minHeight: 0.00, maxHeight: 0.30,
            groundColor: SIMD3<Float>(0.02, 0.10, 0.25)),
    MTBiome(name: "ocean",     minHeight: 0.30, maxHeight: 0.38,
            groundColor: SIMD3<Float>(0.05, 0.25, 0.50)),
    MTBiome(name: "dunes",     minHeight: 0.38, maxHeight: 0.62,
            groundColor: SIMD3<Float>(0.85, 0.65, 0.35)),
    MTBiome(name: "rock",      minHeight: 0.62, maxHeight: 0.80,
            groundColor: SIMD3<Float>(0.55, 0.35, 0.22),
            slopeColor:  SIMD3<Float>(0.40, 0.25, 0.16)),
    MTBiome(name: "peaks",     minHeight: 0.80, maxHeight: 1.00,
            groundColor: SIMD3<Float>(0.75, 0.55, 0.40)),
]
world.config = config   // bumps configVersion → renderer rebuilds

Slope coloring (cliffs)

When a biome sets slopeColor, steep faces use it instead of groundColor. The shader blends by slope angle, so you get natural transitions: grassy hillsides that turn to exposed rock on cliffs, snowy peaks with rocky faces. Only mountain and snowyPeak set it by default.

Tip: if your mountains look "banded" (striped by height), that's the biome bands showing through on steep terrain. Widening the mountain band or strengthening the slope blend fixes it — the shader already forces rock above ~0.65 height on steep slopes.

Biome edges and blending

Vertex colors are computed per-vertex from the height at that vertex, so biome boundaries naturally anti-alias across triangles. Near a boundary the color is blended toward the neighboring biome, which is why shorelines look soft rather than pixelated. At 250-vertex chunk resolution, bands narrower than ~0.02 in height can shimmer — keep functional bands at least that wide.

Best practices

  • Keep bands ordered and non-overlapping unless you specifically want first-match-wins behavior (like the ocean/beach shoreline trick).
  • Mind the sea level. Anything below config.seaLevel (0.45) is underwater — its color only shows through shallow water or on the sea floor at the water's edge.
  • Test with several seeds. A band that looks great on seed 1337 might never appear on a flatter seed. findSafeSpawn() plus a quick flyover on 3–4 seeds is the minimum.
  • Changing biomes is cheap; changing noise is not. Biome edits only recolor vertices (fast rebuild). Noise/config edits regenerate heightfields (slower). Iterate on color first, shape second.
  • Name biomes semantically ("grass", not "band3") — setBiome replaces by name, so good names make overrides predictable.

Removing and resetting

world.removeBiome(named: "ashenFlats")  // built-ins are unaffected
world.resetBiomesToDefault()             // clear ALL customs, restore ladder
world.allBiomes  // the exact lookup order biomeAt consults (customs first)
